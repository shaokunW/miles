# Establish a shared understanding of the game

Use at first engagement, when established intent is fragmented, or after a material direction change. The initial deliverable is a connected explanation that can guide design.

## Understand the whole before deepening a part

Read existing vision, accepted decisions, project records, relevant implementation, and available research. Connect these lenses:

- World and theme; player identity; fantasy; emotional and aesthetic intent.
- Target players, prior knowledge, motivations, access needs, and play situations.
- Core activities, decisions, resources, growth, and supporting-system relationships.
- Camera, space, objects, characters, movement, learned controls, and visual/system language.
- Platform, display, inputs, performance, session and interruption conditions, release scope, and production constraints.

A lens is a source of questions and implications. Develop a sufficient connected model; leave low-impact details for later. A platform or genre name leaves audience behavior and experiential priorities open.

## Contribute a synthesis

Explain how the game creates its intended experience:

> Players [role/audience] inhabit [world/tone]. They repeatedly [activities and choices] to experience [intended satisfaction]. [Supporting systems] contribute through [relationships]. [Delivery conditions] shape [design implications]. Today's task matters because [connection]. These claims come from [sources/status]; [material unknown] remains open.

Treat that wording as a flexible aid. Prefer the game's natural language. Present a concrete player moment when it will help a developer judge the interpretation.

A feature inventory is useful implementation evidence. Add the reasons those activities belong together, how responsibilities are divided, and which tensions require choices.

## Help a developer with limited design experience

Take responsibility for drafting the interpretation. Use already supplied preferences, examples, and facts. Explain two recognizable consequences when priorities remain unclear:

> “Giving the player a personal display moment before service emphasizes making the shop their own. Opening with a completed customer order emphasizes learning to run it. Given the stated focus on service, I would first establish that role, then offer personal display as a choice at the recovery point.”

The recommendation remains conditional on the named evidence. The developer can correct a scene and a priority. Avoid requiring them to define formal concepts or supply a complete design vocabulary.

A targeted question should name the decision it affects. Reuse confirmed answers. Keep requests proportionate and continue useful bounded work when uncertainty remains.

## From shared understanding to an experience model

For broad intentions, read [Experience modeling](experience-modeling.md). Explain the intended goal, a few concrete qualities, the organizing concept, element responsibilities, and resulting rules. The model can expose an unresolved conflict in the project itself.

Show how a condition affects choices. For example, explain how a camera's coverage affects simultaneous awareness of service tasks; leave grip and audience assumptions separately labeled. Investigate structural unknowns before dismissing them as irrelevant.

## Readiness is relative to the task

A broad onboarding plan needs a view of player responsibility, first meaningful success, experiential priority, and the next independent action. A minor hit-target repair can proceed on known layout and access constraints while unrelated creative questions remain open.

Use an existing accepted model directly. During discovery, a missing foundation that could reverse the recommendation calls for conditional options or a targeted question. When questions are disallowed, state the provisional foundation and keep dependent commitments proposed.

Do useful work before claiming broad certainty. A response-length constraint changes how much work is delivered; it leaves the project's confirmation and evidence status intact.

## Onboarding implications

Derive the opening's learning goals from the game promise and the next meaningful decisions. Establish why the player acts, what responsibility is theirs, and what independent behavior would demonstrate understanding. Then select which operations belong now.

A desired capability leaves its tutorial timing open. Adding sale/display choices for personal creations establishes that capability; introducing them before the first customer requires a separate experience decision. Review dependencies and the original tradeoff before changing the whole sequence.

## Persistence

Keep the accepted synthesis and experience model within the existing Project Context. Preserve source, scope, approval, and research status. Goals are accepted intentions; their proposed effects remain hypotheses until tested. Candidate models stay in the current brief or proposed decisions.

Revisit only the affected parts when the direction changes. Reuse the accumulated model instead of restarting discovery. See [Project context](project-context.md) and the [worked discovery](../examples/worked/project-discovery.md).
