# Loops, progression, and pacing

Use this guide for core loops, expansion, unlocks, automation, repetition, and emotional rhythm.

## Map a loop at the relevant scale

Describe the player's intention, action, consequence, and changed situation. A useful loop produces a reason to reconsider or continue. Check what is intrinsically interesting in the activity and what depends on future rewards.

Inspect several timescales only when relevant: an interaction, an encounter or order, a session, and longer progression. Name what the player can accomplish and what remains unresolved at each scale.

A resource increase is a system event. Determine how the player perceives its meaning: new capability, better reliability, more expressive options, visible space, harder choices, greater scale, or a stronger role in the world.

## Unlocks as new possibilities

For an unlock, define the condition, the reveal, the newly available behavior, its prerequisites, and the player's likely next intention. Decide whether it is mandatory, optional, or an invitation for later.

Check affordability and eligibility together. A new opportunity can be visible before purchase is possible when its purpose is aspiration. Its unavailable state then needs a comprehensible path forward. Avoid presenting a required purchase that blocks the only way to earn its cost.

A dependent unlock should respect the actual prerequisite relationship. Distinguish game-level requirements from tutorial sequencing. Keep those rules configurable when the project expects iteration.

## Meaningful repetition

Ask what the player is learning, anticipating, choosing, expressing, or optimizing on another pass. Use variation that serves that role. More clicks or more simultaneous demands can create activity without expanding useful decisions.

Automation can shift responsibility from operating to planning, diagnosis, or customization. Specify what it removes, what agency remains, and what new decisions become available. Test the transition at the audience's tolerated complexity. See [Factorio](../examples/classics/factorio.md) for a transferable question, with explicit boundaries.

## Emotional rhythm

Map intended tension, mastery, relief, surprise, and reflection across a sequence. Label these as aims. Real feelings require player accounts and contextual observation.

Connect escalating demands with opportunities to understand consequences. Make room for consolidation and satisfying endpoints. A long-term invitation can coexist with a completed session; continuation should remain a meaningful choice.

[Journey](../examples/classics/journey.md) offers a lens for companionship and pacing. [Breath of the Wild](../examples/classics/breath-of-the-wild.md) offers a lens for self-chosen destinations. Their patterns need the project's camera, space, movement, and creative goals to support them.

## Validation questions

Can the player explain what has improved? Does the new system create a useful decision? Can they recover when resources or plans go wrong? What does a returning player remember? Do players stop from satisfaction, confusion, interruption, or exhaustion?

Measure those questions separately. Business metrics can inform priorities, while causality and player benefit require appropriate evidence. Keep reward odds, purchase consequences, and time commitments legible when those systems are in scope.

## V2: translate a pacing adjective into design variables

Begin with the intended situation: manageable activity, sustained pressure, suspenseful waiting, deliberate planning, or recovery. Define what that quality means for these players. Then trace its mechanism through arrival patterns, required actions, service effort, concurrent demands, predictability, delay consequences, and opportunities to pause or recover.

“Slower” leaves these relationships unspecified. A longer service action can increase a queue when arrivals are unchanged. A shorter action can increase repetitive input. Treat each adjustment as a hypothesis, with a plausible counter-effect and a scenario to inspect.

A useful handoff distinguishes arrival cadence, task concurrency, input burden, urgency, and recovery. State units and provisional tuning values when needed. Inspect both steady play and bursts. Evaluate startup, ordinary activity, peak demand, completion, and return after interruption.

Connect automation to the responsibility that remains: planning, diagnosis, choice, expression, or expansion. Check whether the resulting play still serves the agreed goal. See [Experience modeling](experience-modeling.md) for the full trace.
