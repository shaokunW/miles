# Worked example: first engagement with a feature-rich prototype

**Original fictional example. The described project records, conversation, acceptance, and decisions are fixtures. Keep them separate from a real game's context.**

## Request

“Improve trial opening so players experience the core interactions and become familiar with the game.”

Available records describe a craft shop with customer service, personal making, ownership, and decoration. Code shows implemented flows and page carriers. There is no shared explanation of the intended relationship between these activities.

## First useful response

> “The current build combines serving customers, making personal pieces, and shaping the room. My working interpretation is a small shop where service creates progress and personal pieces make that progress feel yours. Under that direction, trial opening should first give you a complete service success, then a choice that leaves a visible trace in the shop.
>
> The records confirm the available actions. The priority between learning to host customers and expressing yourself is still open. These lead to different opening moments: a satisfied first customer, or your own first piece displayed before opening. Given your stated emphasis on running the shop, I would begin with the customer success and offer display after a recovery point. Would that opening match the game you picture?”

The question is grounded in consequences. The candidate synthesis performs design work before asking the developer to judge it. Existing platform, input, and camera facts remain reused.

## A fixture continuation

Suppose the developer explicitly accepts service-first and says they want “busy but manageable, with a shop that feels personal.” Miles then extends the model:

Goal: understand the owner's responsibility and independently continue service.

Qualities: understandable work, manageable activity, personal presence.

Concept: pending work asks for attention; completed work leaves room for choice.

Responsibilities: customers express demand; stations show processing state; UI supports necessary information; decor organizes and personalizes space; arrival rules set pressure and recovery.

Rules: distinguish ready-state signals from ordinary decor; offer a stable completion beat; preserve customer/personal item ownership; evaluate arrival concurrency alongside service effort.

These are proposed translations. Explicit acceptance can make them project intentions; their player effects remain untested.

## Now derive the opening

Introduce the customer's independent activity and the owner's service responsibility. Let the player complete finishing, delivery, and reset, with state changes explaining their consequences. At completion, create the agreed recovery point. Use the next slightly varied order to investigate independent understanding.

Keep personal making available according to a separately scoped choice. A full creation-before-opening sequence remains a candidate if the experience priority changes. Implemented page carriers are considered after the player need and required flow are understood.

## A later request

“Let players sell or hang their creations.”

Record the requested capability and ownership implications. Identify dependent decisions: item state, sale consequence, display slots, discoverability, and interruption handling. Revisit opening timing only after explaining the new cost and benefit under the service-first model.

A response can say: “This gives personal work two meaningful futures. I would keep ownership stable until sale or display is confirmed, then introduce those choices at the personal-making completion. Moving that completion before the first customer would extend the opening and change its emphasis; that remains a separate choice.”

## What gets saved

Persist the accepted connected game understanding and service-first priority with scope and source. Preserve accepted goal/quality/concept definitions and reusable state-language rules, labeled as intent with untested causal predictions. Record consequential local opening choices in Decisions. Keep unselected flows and timing candidates in the current brief.

No complete interview is needed on the next task. Miles applies the accumulated model and revisits only changed dependencies.
