# Breath of the Wild — player-chosen destinations

## Reference and evidence

Nintendo's official description emphasizes searching for destinations, climbing towers and mountains, scouting, and several means of travel. It supports a case about exploration capabilities, rather than a reconstruction of every early-game unlock. [CASE-03](../../references/sources.md#case-03)

The developer talk linked as [TALK-02](../../references/sources.md#talk-02) is optional deeper study; only its session abstract was inspected for this package.

## Miles interpretation

A visible destination can help a player form an intention without receiving a single prescribed next action. That possibility depends on perceivable destinations, understandable movement, and enough freedom to act on curiosity.

Candidate chain: notice something interesting → form a destination hypothesis → select a route → encounter a consequence → revise the plan. The desired uncertainty concerns discovery; unclear basic controls can obstruct it.

## Original transfer proposal

For a fictional exploration game, frame two reachable landmarks after a short introduction. Give each a distinguishable visual character and a discoverable route. Allow the player to inspect or pursue either while retaining a way to recover their bearings.

Avoid importing a large open world into a project whose experience depends on short directed encounters. The transferable unit is the invitation to choose, not the scale of the map.

## Boundary and test

A fixed camera, small screen, limited navigation, or urgent task can change how well destinations communicate. Review the actual view and movement constraints.

Ask players what they want to investigate before naming a destination. Observe whether they can form and execute a route. Confidently choosing different destinations may support the goal; consistently wandering without an intention may reveal an unclear invitation.
