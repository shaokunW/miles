# Into the Breach — readable threats, difficult choices

## Reference and evidence

The publisher's description states that enemy attacks are telegraphed in turn-based combat and that civilian buildings matter to the player's power. This supports a discussion of advance information and consequential protection choices. It does not establish perfect determinism for every possible outcome. [CASE-05](../../references/sources.md#case-05)

## Miles interpretation

Making a threat legible can shift effort toward choosing a response. Difficult decisions can remain when several objectives compete and the available actions cannot satisfy them all.

The diagnostic question is which information the intended strategy requires. Hiding that information and asking for a tradeoff are different design choices.

## Original transfer proposal

In a fictional tactical rescue game, preview each revealed hazard's threatened tiles and resolution order. Let players compare a rescue move with a move that protects equipment. State uncertainty explicitly for hazards whose hidden behavior is part of the design.

Keep the preview available through the project's established inspect control. Use an alternative marker or label alongside hazard color. Avoid adding an optimal-move indicator when choosing the response is the central challenge.

## Boundary and test

Some games deliberately depend on hidden information, scouting, or bluffing. Reveal the information the project's rules promise, and preserve uncertainty that gives those mechanics meaning.

Before players commit, ask them to predict the outcome of their selected action. Correct predictions combined with difficult choices can support the intended separation between interface clarity and strategy. Incorrect predictions suggest a communication or rules-model problem worth investigating.

**Persistent knowledge:** a rule about threat previews belongs to the relevant mode or system only after confirmation.
