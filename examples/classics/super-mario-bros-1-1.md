# Super Mario Bros. — playable variation

## Reference and evidence

Lars Doucet's original analysis describes an early Goomba encounter and variations involving confined terrain and multiple enemies. He uses them to illustrate a gradual learning sequence. This is a designer's reading of the game, not a cited Nintendo production account or controlled player study. [CASE-01](../../references/sources.md#case-01)

## Miles interpretation

A useful question is whether the player gets to apply a concept in a changed situation before several new demands compete. Passing one encounter may indicate successful execution while leaving general understanding uncertain.

Candidate chain: perceive an obstacle → attempt the learned movement → observe the consequence → recognize a related obstacle → adapt the action. Each proposed player interpretation remains a hypothesis.

## Original transfer proposal

For a fictional crafting game, teach a placement tool with one meaningful piece, then offer a different shape that needs the same operation. Later combine placement with a second mechanic. Keep the first mistake cheap to correct and make the control discoverable for the intended audience.

The design question is what evidence justifies reducing assistance. A fixed count of three encounters is unnecessary.

## Boundary and test

A platforming convention familiar to experienced players can be unfamiliar to a new audience or different input device. Check the prerequisite control knowledge before relying on an encounter to teach it.

Observe a fresh player applying the operation to a changed piece without the original highlight. Record mistakes and assistance. Revise if the player repeats the demonstrated sequence successfully but cannot recognize the same opportunity elsewhere.

**Persistent knowledge:** only a developer-confirmed teaching convention with an explicit project scope belongs in context. The reference game's pattern remains a reference.
