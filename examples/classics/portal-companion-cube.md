# Portal — recovery, narrative, and mechanical purpose

Spoiler scope: the Companion Cube encounter and its ending.

## Reference and evidence

The developers' postmortem describes revising an encounter that caused cube loss and frustrating backtracking. It also describes environmental guidance followed by dialogue, and an incineration ending that prepared a later mechanical interaction. These are documented development choices. [CASE-02](../../references/sources.md#case-02)

## Miles interpretation

The useful diagnosis distinguishes reasoning about a puzzle from repeated recovery work that interrupts it. Guidance can combine spatial evidence with concise language when both serve the intended encounter. A mechanically important object also gives narrative framing something the player actively uses.

These are analytical lessons; the source does not establish that the same combination will work for every game.

## Original transfer proposal

In a fictional repair game, let players carry a damaged instrument through several useful repair steps. When a failed step drops the instrument, return it to a nearby safe position. Preserve the repair decision while reducing the walk needed to retry.

Teach the relevant station through its visible state and the established interaction cue. Provide a brief optional explanation if the player cannot identify the station. Keep any essential spoken information available in an appropriate alternative channel.

## Boundary and test

The instrument must remain useful to the player's objective; dialogue alone cannot establish that purpose. Removing every obstacle would also remove the repair challenge.

Observe where players spend effort: choosing the repair, locating the interaction, or repeating recovery travel. A successful change should preserve the intended decision while reducing the specific recovery bottleneck. Ask what the instrument means to the player rather than claiming a guaranteed attachment.

**Persistent knowledge:** an accepted rule for recovery or guidance needs its own project scope and confirmation.
